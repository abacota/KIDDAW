# KIDDAW: the plan

KIDDAW is a free music studio that runs in the browser on school Chromebooks.
A kindergartner can make a beat in a minute, and a fifth grader who gets hooked can keep going until they're making sophisticated music.
It is organized by the **elements of music** students learn in class: rhythm, melody, harmony, texture, timbre, dynamics and form.
It uses music-class language throughout: ta and ta-ti, solfege, standard Boomwhacker colors, forte and piano, AB and ABA.
It is its own app, separate from Kingdom of Sound.

## The big idea: built for kids, not adults

BeepBox, BandLab and Online Sequencer are good tools, but they are built for what adults need.
Kids get the same adult tool in brighter colors. KIDDAW is built for what kids need:

| Adult tools give you | What a kid needs instead |
|---|---|
| A blank screen to figure out | Sound from the first tap, and something to start from and change |
| EQ, compressors, decibels | Songs that sound good automatically |
| "Delay", "velocity", "quantize" | Words from music class: echo, forte and piano, steady beat |
| Tiny buttons and menus | Big targets, touch, everything in view |
| Every feature at once | New tools that appear as the student grows |
| Lots of reading | Colors, icons and sounds, with very little reading for K-2 |
| A big grid to fill with your eyes | Music you shape with your ears |

**For kids, power means musical control, not technical control.** Students get deep control over the music:
syncopation, harmony, countermelodies, dynamics, texture and form.
KIDDAW quietly handles the engineering: balancing the layers, keeping it from distorting, and keeping the sound clean.
A student never sees a mixer, a decibel or an EQ.

The design rule comes from Scratch: **a low floor** (anyone can start), **a high ceiling** (no limit on how good the music gets),
and **wide walls** (beats, melodies, harmony, sound design, whole songs).

## Ears first, not eyes

What Adam has seen with BeepBox, BandLab and Online Sequencer: students use too much.
They add all the notes, every layer and every effect, and they click with their eyes, not their ears.
A pattern that looks good on the grid wins over one that sounds good.

So KIDDAW is built to make students listen:

- **You always hear what you change.** The part you're working on loops while you edit, so every change sounds right away, in context.
- **Play it first.** Students make music by playing: tapping a beat on the pads, playing a melody on Boomwhacker-colored keys.
  KIDDAW writes down what they played in ta and ta-ti and solfege. Then they fix it up. Ears go first and the notation follows.
- **Small canvases.** Four or eight beats at a time, and only the notes at the student's level. No giant empty grid begging to be filled.
- **Limits set by level.** A K-1 song has a few layers, not twenty. Rests count as music.
- **Listen and choose.** Try a change, hear the song both ways, and keep the one that sounds better.
- **The Listen button.** It hides the screen and plays the whole song, so students listen with nothing to look at.
  Before sharing, a song gets one full listen-through.
- **Composing challenges.** Adam writes challenges with limits ("8 beats, only sol, mi and la, end on sol") in plain text.
  Limits make students choose, and choosing makes them listen.

## Who uses it, and how

Every student has their own Chromebook and signs in through Clever.

| Setting | What it needs |
|---|---|
| Whole class, each on their own Chromebook | Runs well on a slow Chromebook, 25 students at once on school wifi |
| Stations or centers, often in pairs | Big buttons two students can share; quick to start, quick to save |
| Adam on the projector, class composes together | Projector mode: big, readable from the back of the room |

## How a song looks

A song is one screen. **Sections go across** (A, B, A) and **layers go down** (a beat, a melody, a harmony part).
That one picture shows two elements at once: form (the order of sections) and texture (how many layers are playing).

- Tap a box to open its editor: beat boxes for a rhythm layer, solfege rows in Boomwhacker colors for a melody layer.
- Tap a layer's instrument to change its sound.
- Dynamics marks (piano, forte, crescendo) sit right on the sections.

Every tool is labeled with its element, using that element's icon and color, so students always know which element they're working with.
"Today we're working on texture" points to something on the screen.

## The seven ladders

Each element has a ladder of steps. A student's level decides how far up each ladder they can go.
**One song, many views:** every song is stored at full power, so a kindergartner's beat can grow into a fifth grader's song with nothing lost.

First draft, for Adam to adjust. The grade band in parentheses is where a step first opens.

**Rhythm** (with steady beat and tempo)
1. Steady beat; ta and rest; fast and slow (K-1)
2. ta-ti (K-1)
3. ta-ah; meter in 2 and 4 (2-3)
4. ta-ka-ti-ki (2-3)
5. ta-ti-ki and ta-ka-ti; meter in 3; largo, andante, allegro (4-5)
6. Syncopation and dotted rhythms (syllables to confirm with Adam); accelerando and ritardando (4-5)
7. Swing, 6/8, triplets, any rhythm (Studio)

**Melody**
1. sol-mi (K-1)
2. sol-mi-la (K-1)
3. do-re-mi, then do-re-mi-sol-la (pentatonic) (2-3)
4. Low la, low sol, high do (2-3)
5. fa and ti: the full major scale (4-5)
6. Minor and other keys (4-5)
7. Every note, any key or mode (Studio)

**Harmony**
1. A drone: do and sol held under a melody (2-3)
2. An ostinato: a short pattern that repeats underneath (2-3)
3. Two chords: I and V (4-5)
4. Three chords: I, IV and V; a bass line (4-5)
5. Any chords, including minor chords (Studio)

**Texture**
1. Thin and thick: add and remove layers (K-1)
2. Melody and accompaniment (2-3)
3. Layers coming in and dropping out: build-ups and breakdowns (2-3)
4. Rounds: the same melody starting again later (4-5)
5. A countermelody: two melodies at once (4-5)
6. Balance: choose which layer is in front (Studio)

**Timbre**
1. Choose instruments by family: percussion, Orff, keyboard, strings, winds, voices, electronic; a drum kit or body percussion (K-1)
2. Free play: play any instrument live, no song needed (K-1)
3. Shape a sound in kid words, like bright or dark, short or long; effects like echo, big room, robot, underwater (2-3)
4. Record your own sound (your voice, a found sound) and play it as an instrument (4-5)
5. Design a sound from scratch (Studio)

**Dynamics**
1. Forte and piano (K-1)
2. pp to ff (2-3)
3. Crescendo and decrescendo (2-3)
4. Accents; one layer louder or softer than the others (4-5)
5. Draw the dynamics across a whole song (Studio)

**Form**
1. Same and different: A and B (K-1)
2. ABA (2-3)
3. Rondo (ABACA); intro and coda (4-5)
4. Verse and chorus; theme and variations (A, A', A'') (4-5)
5. Any form; copy and change sections freely (Studio)

## Levels, no accounts

Each level gets its own link, for example `kiddaw.vercel.app/k-1`. A level opens every step marked for it and every step below.
The **Studio** level opens everything, and Adam can give its link to any student who's ready, in any grade.
Adam can also make a link for one element, so a dynamics lesson opens with the dynamics tools ready.

Adam puts the links on his Clever teacher page, so students tap an icon instead of typing.
The ladders and levels live in two plain text files Adam can edit without code:
`settings/ladders.txt` (the steps of each element) and `settings/levels.txt` (how many beats and sounds each level gets).

## Sounds

Quality sounds are what make a student's music feel real.

- Drum kits: acoustic, electronic and more
- Body percussion: stomp, pat, clap, snap
- Sampled instruments in families: piano, strings, guitar, bass, winds, voices, and Orff instruments (xylophone, metallophone, glockenspiel)
- Synths: chip sounds like BeepBox, plus pads, leads and bass
- Free sample sets with open licenses. Each sound loads only when a song uses it, so 25 Chromebooks on school wifi aren't kept waiting.

## Saving and sharing

Each student has their own Chromebook, so songs saved on it stay with that student. No accounts, and nothing about students stored online.

- **Auto-save** after every change. A "My songs" shelf holds all of a student's songs.
- **Song link.** The Share button makes a link that contains the whole song. A student posts it in Google Classroom,
  and Adam opens it on the projector to play it for the class. It is also a backup if a Chromebook gets replaced.
- **Song file.** Songs with recordings are too big for a link, so they save as a file to the student's Google Drive.
- **Download my song** as an audio file.

To test early: some districts set Chromebooks to erase saved data when a student signs out.

## How it's built: five parts, each with one job

| Part | Its one job |
|---|---|
| Song | What a song is, stored at full power: sections, layers, patterns, notes, sounds, dynamics. Makes and reads song links. No sound, no screens. |
| Sound engine | Plays a song on Tone.js's clock. Samples, synths and effects, plus the automatic engineering (balance, no distortion). Loads sounds only when needed. Pauses when the tab is hidden. |
| Tools | The screens, labeled by element. They change the song. They never make sound or keep time themselves. |
| Saving | Auto-save, My songs, song links, song files. The only part that touches storage. |
| Settings | The ladders, levels and element links. Plain text Adam can edit. |

The rule that matters most: **screens change the song; the engine plays it.**
In the old version the beat timer lived inside screen code, which is why Play stops after one beat.

Tools: keep React, TypeScript, Tone.js and Vercel. They are good choices. The problem was how the app was built, not what it was built with.

## How we keep it from breaking

1. **Build in one place:** here, saved to GitHub. AI Studio retires from KIDDAW so two tools never overwrite each other.
2. **An automatic check before anything goes live:** type check, tests on the song data
   (a song link turns back into the exact same song; "ti-ti" never appears), and a full build. It runs on every save to GitHub.
3. **A test link and a student link.** New work goes to a test version first, and Adam tries it on a real student Chromebook.
   `kiddaw.vercel.app` only changes when Adam says so.
4. **Small steps.** Each change does one job, gets checked, then gets saved. No "rebuild the whole app" requests.
5. **Test on the real thing.** A student Chromebook, with a big song playing, has to keep steady time.

## What happens to the old code

A fresh foundation in the same GitHub project, so the `kiddaw.vercel.app` link stays the same.
We keep what works: the drum and instrument sounds and the kid-friendly track names.
The old version stays in GitHub's history, and the live site keeps showing it until Phase 1 replaces it.
